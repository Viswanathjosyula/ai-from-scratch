How to use it

Study one cluster until you can say the exit check out loud. Then move. Keep the roadmap’s three daily threads. Use a cluster to choose what the architecture and management minutes cover once the matching module is underway.

Open this clusterWhen
1. Math and ML foundationsBeside Modules 1–2. The ceiling is small. Do not start a separate math course first.
2. Deep learningBeside Module 3.
3. LLMs, RAG, agents, evals, fine-tuningBeside Modules 4–5, then keep going. The site stops early.
4. Data, features, labelingLeakage rows beside Module 1. The rest beside Module 6.
5. Software engineering for ML systemsWhen Module 7’s serving sketch feels familiar.
6. Architecture and system designBeside Modules 7–8. The Salesforce subsection is one context inside this cluster.
7. MLOps, security, privacy, governance, riskDrift and rollout beside Module 7. Governance rows deepen toward the director checkpoint.
8. Management and director skillsShort stakeholder reps can start immediately, as the roadmap already says. Work the manager rows at the manager checkpoint and the director rows at the director checkpoint.

Level can be more than one. Architect means you can design and defend the choice. Manager means you can staff it, gate it, and run the failure. Director means you can fund it, stop it, or govern it.

Site is the live curriculum: modules 1–8, interview.html, projects.html, and FraudWatch.

Where the site is already strong

Hold these. They are the spine, and later clusters sit on them.

  • What learning from examples is, and when a heuristic is enough (Module 1).
  • Splits, overfitting, leakage, and metrics chosen for the business, including why accuracy fails on rare fraud (Modules 1 and 6, FraudWatch).
  • Linear and logistic regression, trees, bagging, boosting, and when a tabular model beats a deep net (Module 2).
  • Neuron, loss, gradient step, backprop as an explanation, regularization (Module 3).
  • Tokens, QKV attention, the transformer block, next-token pretraining, SFT, RLHF/DPO, temperature and context (Module 4).
  • Prompt vs RAG vs fine-tune vs a single tool call, RAG failure modes, a first eval list (Module 5).
  • Feature timing, label delay, chunking, metadata filters (Module 6).
  • Batch vs online vs streaming, versioning, shadow and canary, drift types (Module 7).
  • The 6-box design and two worked examples (Module 8).

Biggest gaps

These are the holes a hiring loop can open after the site feels finished.

  1. Engineering a change. Tests for data, features, models, and prompts. Data contracts. An eval that blocks a release. SLOs. Idempotent tool actions that write records.
  2. Agents past one tool call. Orchestration, memory, a tool registry, model routing, and what happens when a tool has a side effect.
  3. Retrieval past the pipeline sketch. Hybrid search, reranking, query rewriting, permission filters that survive a cache, and embedding-index attacks.
  4. Adaptation and cost. LoRA and other parameter-efficient fine-tuning, when a reasoning model is worth the latency, quantization and routing as cost levers.
  5. A control system. NIST’s Govern / Map / Measure / Manage, the OWASP LLM risk list as controls, privacy, residency, fairness slices, red-team gates, vendor diligence.
  6. System shape. Multi-tenant isolation, build vs buy vs platform, exit cost, and a Salesforce estate (Agentforce, Data Cloud, the trust layer) placed on that shape. The site does not teach the Salesforce products. The career plan asks for sketches. The products themselves are still study.
  7. Running the work. Hiring screens, incident reviews, portfolio intake, investment and stop memos, org design, stage gates. The interview bank is a technical drill.

Shelf

Leave these unless a specific role demands them. The career roadmap already shelves the same idea: depth on FraudWatch and one RAG system beats a tour of every model family.

  • Proof-heavy linear algebra, convex optimization, and information theory past cross-entropy.
  • CNN families, detection, and segmentation past Module 3’s one page.
  • Diffusion, GAN, and VAE internals. Module 5 only names them.
  • Reinforcement-learning control theory (MDPs, policy iteration). Preference tuning is in cluster 3. Control theory is not the job.
  • Training-cluster design and custom model architectures.
  • LoadCast and MarketSense, unless an energy or markets interview appears.

1. Math and ML foundations

What the roles actually test: you can pick a metric, smell a leak, and explain a threshold. Directors will not ask you to derive backprop. They will ask why a reported lift is not real.

Study from: Google Machine Learning Crash Course (regression through classification, datasets, overfitting); DeepLearning.AI Machine Learning Specialization, Course 1 and the “advice for applying machine learning” week; Stanford CS229’s supervised core (the lecture notes’ early chapters, not the control-theory chapters).

Exit check. In five minutes, using a fraud queue: why accuracy lies, where a leak would enter, and how you would set a threshold from cost.

2. Deep learning

Enterprise architect interviews test judgment about nets, not a vision career. Module 3 is the right depth for the mechanism. The gaps are debugging a run, transfer, and the cost conversation.

Study from: Module 3 first; Google Crash Course neural-network and embeddings modules; fast.ai Practical Deep Learning for the “train, then look inside” habit; CS224N week 2 (backprop and neural-net basics) if the chain rule still feels verbal.

Exit check. Explain a neuron, one gradient step, and why a boosted tree can beat a net on tabular fraud. Say what you would inspect if train loss fell and validation loss rose.

3. LLMs, RAG, agents, evals, fine-tuning

This is the cluster where current AI architect postings (Accenture LLM architect, Cognizant agentic architect, EXL principal architect, Deloitte Google AI architect) go far past Module 5.

Study from: Modules 4 and 5 first; CS224N weeks 3–6 (transformers, pretraining, post-training, PEFT, agents and RAG, benchmarking); CS229’s LLM lectures (tokenization through RAG, fine-tuning, safety); CS324’s adaptation and societal lists only for the risk names, with the depth in cluster 7.

Exit check. One sitting: attention, a RAG pipeline with two failure modes, and a choice among prompt, RAG, fine-tune, and tools. Then say who owns the eval set and what number blocks release.

4. Data, features, labeling

Chip Huyen’s Designing Machine Learning Systems spends chapters on data engineering, training data, and features because that is where production systems break. Module 6 is the right introduction. The gaps are contracts, point-in-time joins, feature stores, lineage, and data readiness as a funding test.

Study from: Module 6; Google Crash Course numerical and categorical data modules; Full Stack Deep Learning’s data-management lecture; Made With ML’s data and systems-design lessons.

6. Architecture and system design

Module 8’s 6-box is the whiteboard skeleton. Staff-plus architect work, in Will Larson’s terms, is the technical direction around that skeleton: requirements that are expensive to change, paved roads, and build-versus-buy. Salesforce sits at the end of this cluster as one estate, not as the map.

Study from: Module 8; Chip Huyen chapters 1, 2, and 7 (requirements, framing, deployment); CMU Machine Learning in Production, “Thinking like a Software Architect”; Made With ML systems design (batch versus online).

7. MLOps, security, privacy, governance, risk

Module 7 covers the production loop, drift, and a short LLMOps list. Architect posts ask for the operating system around that loop. Director posts ask for a risk framework a legal or security partner recognizes. NIST’s language is Govern, Map, Measure, Manage. OWASP’s 2025 list is the application-security checklist.

Study from: Module 7; NIST AI RMF 1.0 (the four functions, not the full catalog); OWASP Top 10 for LLM Applications 2025; NIST’s Generative AI profile for the risk names; Chip Huyen chapters 8–10 (shift, continual learning, infrastructure).

Exit check. Five risks for one system. Each row has a harmed party, a detection, a control, and the gap you still accept. At least three rows map to a named OWASP item.

8. Management and director skills

Topic list for the management thread. The interview bank does not cover this job. Full Stack Deep Learning’s “ML teams” lecture and Camille Fournier’s The Manager’s Path (chapters on managing people, a team, several teams, and senior leadership) are the public skeletons. Director postings add portfolio intake, value realization, and governance forums.

Staff-plus behavior, from Will Larson’s Staff Engineer, belongs at architect level: write the strategy, curate quality, show up for the irreversible decision, and grow owners.

Exit check, manager. A six-week pilot with users, metric, responsibilities, rollback, and a kill rule. A hiring screen with one answer you would reject. A one-page incident with an owner and a new test.
Exit check, director. One use case you would fund and one you would stop, with evidence. A quarter’s cadence: what is reviewed, what is killed, which role you would hire.
